Ten marks

Fig. 01 · Two lightsMetaphor fusion

Two circles, two lighting modes; the overlap is the image. The intersection is a vesica, so the flame in the name survives without being drawn.

Fig. 02 · SeatProduct action

The housing seated over the camera array: one lens, one emitter, one plate. The most literal mark here, and the most obviously hardware.

Fig. 03 · VesicaNegative space

A lens in section, pointed at both ends, with the aperture cut out of the centre. Reads as an optic and as an eye, and holds the flame in the name without drawing one.

Fig. 04 · LocusProduct action

A lesion located rather than described. The patented spatial mapping, reduced to a registration mark. Reads as an instrument reticle, not a target.

Fig. 05 · Beam FMonogram + meaning

An F cut out of a solid plate, its upper arm running off the edge as emitted light. Works as an app icon at any size and tiles as a pattern.

Fig. 06 · Two platesProduct action

The same lesion, twice, at identical size: the comparison view that friction currently prevents. Says what the product does and claims nothing about the result.

Fig. 07 · Two modesMetaphor fusion

One aperture, half lit: polarized and unpolarized, the button that alternates them. The most compact mark of the set and the strongest at 16px.

Fig. 08 · Scale FConstruction geometry

The F as a measurement rule, ticks alternating long and short. Argues resolution without stating a figure the brand has not yet quantified.

Fig. 09 · LogotypeNo mark

Wordmark and a caliper rule, nothing else. The quietest option and the hardest to get wrong; needs a separate app icon, for which Fig. 05 is the natural partner.

Fig. 10 · f monogramMonogram + meaning

A drawn lowercase f whose terminal ends in a light source. Softer than the rest, included as the warm end of the range for the consumer product.

Taglines

  1. A
    Repeatable light.

    The engineering claim, and the only one the product has actually been designed for. Reads as a spec to a clinician and as a promise of consistency to everyone else.

  2. B
    Find it again.

    Spatial mapping and follow-up in three words. No interpretation, no verdict, and the same sentence works in an exam room and on a patient's arm.

  3. C
    The same light, every visit.

    Repeatability said plainly. Longer, warmer, and the safest line to put in front of someone who is frightened.

  4. D
    A record, not a verdict.

    States the regulatory limit as the offer rather than the disclaimer. Strongest line for the consumer product; check it with counsel before it ships.

  5. E
    Made to be compared.

    Points at the image quality argument without asserting a number. Works next to a DermLite without naming one.
