Four things to show.

Two ways to represent each. Every grey hatched box is a slot: the label on it is the shot to take and the ratio it needs. Send the photographs and they drop straight in. Photographic groups sit on the dark plate, which is where §5.1 already puts captures; the ramp stays off anything real.

Resolution

R1 — the magnification ladder

One capture, three crops, each deeper than the last, with the scale bar shrinking under them. It proves the detail is there rather than asserting a number, and it needs no competitor in the frame.

Read the fine print.

No added lens. The resolving power comes from the phone's own sensor and the software behind it, so what the housing contributes is light and distance, held the same every time.

Alignment

A1 — two dates, then both at once

March, September, and the two laid over each other. The third frame is the whole argument: if registration works, the overlay is sharp, and if it does not, it ghosts. The offset readout underneath is what the app actually corrected.

It finds the frame for you.

Point the housing at a spot you have photographed before and the app registers the new frame against the old one, correcting for rotation and offset before the shutter fires. You are not asked to hold still and guess.

Colour

C1 — three rooms, one result

The strongest of the eight, if you can shoot it. Same spot photographed under three different room lights on the top row, visibly different; the same three normalised on the bottom row, indistinguishable. Nothing has to be explained.

Colour you can trust twice.

The housing supplies its own light, so the room stops mattering. A known reference in the frame lets the app normalise every capture to the same white point, which is the only way a colour difference between two dates means a difference in the skin rather than a difference in the bulb overhead.

Tracking

T1 — the map and the run of dates

A body map with pins on one side, and the captures for one pin laid out along a dated line on the other. Shows both halves of what tracking is: where it was, and when.

Find it again.

Every capture is pinned to a place on the body, not to a filename. Months later the phone's camera shows you where a spot was, on the skin in front of you, and takes the follow-up from the same position.

T2 — the marker on the skin

The AR view, held over an arm, with the marker sitting where the spot is and a date chip on it. One photograph, and it is the only one of the eight that shows the product being used.

Find it again.

Point the phone at the area and the marker is already there, on the skin in front of you, waiting to take the follow-up from the same position. A map and a record, and nothing on the screen is a score or an opinion.
