Flacara · explorations · round 03

Naming & the brand hero

Plum #3B3350 is in. flacara.css and the token tables in DESIGN_SYSTEM.md both carry it now, so everything below is drawn in the new accent. This sheet covers the two new questions: what the devices are called, and what home looks like once the hardware leaves it.

§1 — “flacara unulC” / “flacara unulP”

The instinct is right: one brand, two devices, a shared first name. The origin is right too: flacără is a flame and unul is one, so the first generation names itself. What does not survive contact is the trailing capital.

  • 01 · Collision

    C means the opposite thing to each audience

    Internally C is Clinical and P is Patient. To a dermatologist reading a purchase order, C is Consumer. The one letter carrying the whole distinction is the one letter that inverts depending on who is holding the box. A naming scheme cannot have its load-bearing character be ambiguous.

  • 02 · Rank

    It brands the patient device as the lesser SKU

    unulC and unulP are visibly the same product with a different letter, which invites the question “what did I not get?”, and the honest answer is a different regulatory class, not a worse camera. Tiering by letter forces you to keep answering that question.

  • 03 · Speech

    Nobody can say it, spell it, or search it

    “OO-nool see” or “UN-ul pee”; screen readers say “unul cee”; support calls will hear “unulsee”, “unal C”, “Unil-P”. Lowercase word plus trailing capital also breaks every house style guide and most procurement systems.

  • 04 · Roadmap

    Counting in Romanian stops working at three

    Unul, doi, trei, then patru, and “Flacara PatruC” is not a product. Worse, doiC does not read as a successor to unulC to anyone who does not speak Romanian; it reads as an unrelated device.

  • 05 · Mark

    Only one word here is registrable

    Single letters and generic numerals are weak marks. You would be protecting “Flacara” and “Unul” and nothing else, which means the C/P distinction, the part doing all the work, is the part you cannot defend.

Five alternatives, each shown as the pair. The test I applied: can a receptionist say it down a phone, does the clinical one obviously outrank the consumer one without insulting it, and does it still work at generation four.

01
flacaraProspecialist
flacaraconsumer

Pro, and the unsuffixed brand

The consumer device is simply Flacara. That is the strongest available signal that the brand is the brand and the professional unit is a variant of it, not the other way round. Pro is understood in every market without translation, and it never has to be explained on a phone call. Roadmap is Pro 2, Pro 3, on forever.

Cost: it is the least distinctive option on this sheet. “Pro” is what everyone does. You are trading ownership for zero friction, which is the right trade for a company between prototype and pilot.

02
flacaraClinicspecialist
flacaraHomeconsumer

Named for where it lives

Says setting, not rank, so neither device is the lesser one. It also does regulatory work for free: Home quietly implies not-diagnostic without a disclaimer, and Clinic sets the expectation that the other unit is a professional instrument. Best fit for the “a record, not a verdict” line.

Cost: boxes you in if the consumer unit ever gets used in a clinic, or the pro unit in a house call. Both happen.

03
flacaraXPspecialist
flacaraOneconsumer

The spec becomes the name

XP is cross-polarization: a real capability, already the word clinicians use, and it earns the pro position by describing a feature rather than asserting a tier. One is “unul” in English — the origin story survives translation.

Cost: only works if XP genuinely stays exclusive to the clinical unit. If the consumer device ships cross-polarized too, the name is a lie and you cannot take it back.

04
flacaraUnulspecialist
flacaraLumenconsumer

Keep the Romanian, drop the letter

If “unul” is non-negotiable, make it a name rather than a stem. Two real words distinguish the devices instead of one character, and both belong to the same world: flame, one, light. The most ownable pair here by a distance.

Cost: nothing tells a buyer which one outranks the other. You would be spending marketing budget to teach the hierarchy that option 01 gets for free.

05
flacara100specialist
flacara10consumer

An instrument series

How the category actually names itself — DermLite runs DL1 through DL5. Numbers rank without insulting, scale forever, and read as equipment rather than as a gadget. Sits comfortably beside a spec table.

Cost: cold, forgettable, and hostile to the consumer product. A frightened person does not want to buy a Flacara 10.

If it were mine: 01 for what ships, with Unul kept as the internal generation codename, so the story stays available for the brand page without being load-bearing on a box. 02 is the close second and the better answer if legal wants the consumer device to disclaim itself in its own name. Whatever you pick, decide it before the pre-order form goes live. A SKU name in someone's order confirmation is very expensive to change.

§2 — Home, with the hardware taken out

Agreed on removing the outline: once there are two devices, a picture of one of them on the home page is a claim about which one matters. What replaces it depends on what you think home is for. Three answers, in increasing order of how much work the page does.

Home · H1 — Aperture

brand as light
flacara

Repeatable light.

The ramp becomes concentric apertures rather than a bar: still the mark's own gradient, still §2.6's first permitted place, and it says “optics” without drawing a single piece of hardware. Closest in spirit to what the page has now, minus the device.

Against it: a centred lockup on a glowing ring is the most generic composition in this document. It is beautiful and it is also what every seed-stage hardware company's home page looks like. If you build it, the concentric rings have to be geometrically derived from the mark, not decorative, or it reads as wallpaper.

Home · H2 — The fork

recommended
flacara

Repeatable light, for two very different rooms.

For specialists Clinicians The professional instrument, and the comparison against a DermLite DL4. For everyone else Patients A clear picture of a spot on your skin, and another one later.

Once the devices live on their own pages, home's real job is the brand plus the fork, and a visitor who lands here is already one of two people. This is the only direction that admits that. It also solves the naming problem structurally: the two doors do the tiering, so the product names can stay quiet.

Against it: a page that only routes is a page that sells nothing, and you lose the full-bleed brand moment entirely. Mitigation is to let the lockup occupy the top two-thirds and keep the doors as a band at the fold, which is what is drawn here, but it needs the outline trace or something equivalent above it or the top half is empty.

Home · H3 — Wordmark alone

maximum restraint
flacara

Repeatable light.

The current hero with the drawing deleted and nothing put back. Costs an hour, cannot be wrong, and the enormous empty field is a legitimate choice for an instrument brand. It reads as confidence rather than as an unfinished page, but only if the type is set at the size drawn here.

Against it: it is a placeholder that happens to look good. Ship it as a stopgap while H2 gets built, not as the answer.

§3 — Clinicians, and what “pro” actually means here

The reflex for a pro device is a dark hero, and §2.2 fences dark surfaces to image-viewing contexts: the mat exists so a photograph is judged under a known condition, not so marketing can look serious. C1 breaks that rule on purpose; C2 gets the same read without touching it. Both are drawn so you can decide whether the rule is worth an exception.

Clinicians · C1 — Dark instrument

breaks §2.2

Built for the exam room.

Cross-polarized and unpolarized in one pass, at a fixed working distance, so the image you take today can be compared to the one you take in six months.

ModesXP / non-XP
ReferenceDermLite DL4
StagePrototype → pilot

The outline in near-white on --fl-mat-deep, which is exactly how the device will be seen when a clinician is actually looking at images. It is the most convincingly professional thing in this document.

Against it: §2.2 says dark is a viewing condition, not a mood. Use it here and the mat stops meaning “controlled surround” and starts meaning “premium”, which quietly devalues it everywhere it does real work. If you want this, amend §2.2 explicitly to permit one dark hero on the clinical page. Do not just do it.

Clinicians · C2 — Elevation

recommended

A fixed geometry, two lighting modes, and the same framing every visit. The repeatability is the instrument.

Pro by measurement rather than by darkness: the outline treated as an engineering elevation, dimension lines called out, a mono spec strip under the headline. Specialists respond to the drawing being dimensioned: it signals the thing was designed to a tolerance. Stays entirely inside the system.

Against it: it is quieter than C1 and will lose a side-by-side first-impression test. It wins the second look, which is the one that places an order.

§4 — Patients

Same outline, opposite handling. On the clinical page it is an instrument drawn to a dimension; here it should be an object of a known, unthreatening size, or absent entirely.

Patients · P1 — In the hand

recommended

A clear picture.

It photographs a spot on your skin, closely and the same way each time. It does not tell you what the spot is.

ACTUAL SIZE · FITS IN ONE HAND

The device drawn smaller than on the clinical page, with a scale rule instead of dimension lines, the same asset saying “this is small and it is yours” rather than “this is calibrated”. The size difference between the two pages is doing the tiering work that the product names then do not have to.

Against it: a scale bar is still instrument language. If user testing says it reads clinical, cut the bar and let the drawing sit at hand size on its own.

Patients · P2 — One spot, two dates

alternate · needs real imagery

Find it again.

A clear picture now, and another one later, so you can see whether anything changed. No score, no verdict.

Carried over from round 02 and still the strongest statement of what the product is for. Shows the outcome instead of the hardware, which is arguably more correct for this audience than P1.

Against it: the two plates are the same file. It cannot ship until there is a genuine second photograph of the same lesion, and faking one would be exactly the claim the page spends its next section disowning.
